1. ONE-LINE SUMMARY
======================================================================
The US has no single federal AI law, so every state writes its own.
This product shows who the rules apply to, what each one requires,
and who is breaking them, for three different users.

Analogy: like sales tax. Every state sets its own rules, and a business
selling everywhere has to follow all of them at once.

3. THE PROBLEM AND EVIDENCE (verified 7 Nov 2026)
======================================================================
Items marked [UNCHECKED] have not been re-verified; keep them off
slides until they are.

SCALE OF THE PROBLEM
- In 2025, state lawmakers in all 50 states introduced 1,208 AI bills;
  about 145 were enacted (MultiState count; NCSL counts 131 because it
  defines "AI-related" differently).
  Source: https://www.multistate.ai/artificial-intelligence-ai-legislation
- That is nearly double 2024 (635 bills introduced, 99 enacted).
  Source: same MultiState page
- By March 2026, 45 states had already introduced 1,561 AI bills, more
  than all of 2024.
  Source: https://ai2.work/blog/state-ai-bills-surge-past-1-500-as-congress-remains-deadlocked
- One AI hiring tool can face several state and city rules at once
  (e.g. Illinois, New York City, Colorado, Texas).

FEDERAL VS STATE
- Executive Order 14365 (Dec 11, 2025) told the Justice Department to
  set up an AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state AI laws; the
  Attorney General created it Jan 9, 2026. An executive order cannot
  override state law by itself, so companies must keep complying until
  a court blocks a law.
  Sources: https://www.lawandtheworkplace.com/2026/04/what-president-trumps-ai-executive-order-14365-means-for-employers/
           https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/trump-ai-executive-order-and-state-law-preemption-what-to-do-now
- The Task Force's first court involvement was in xAI's lawsuit against
  Colorado (2026), not a standalone DOJ lawsuit.
  Source: https://gc.ai/blog/state-ai-laws

EXAMPLE STATE LAWS
- Illinois HB 3773 (in effect Jan 1, 2026): bans AI with a
  discriminatory effect in hiring, promotion, firing and other
  employment decisions (including using ZIP codes as a proxy); employers
  must notify workers and applicants when AI is used. The state's
  implementing rules were still delayed as of mid-2026.
  Sources: https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/illinois_enacts_artificial_intelligence_law_focused_employment_practices_0824.html
           https://natlawreview.com/article/states-take-aim-ai-employment-decisions-illinoiss-disclosure-and-anti
- California SB 53 (in force Jan 1, 2026): frontier model developers
  must publish safety frameworks and report serious safety incidents;
  heaviest duties for developers with $500M+ revenue. Almost never
  applies to a small business.
  [UNCHECKED] penalties up to $1M per violation.
  Source: https://www.vaquill.ai/blog/california-ai-laws-explained-sb-53-and-the-rest
- California AB 2013 (in force Jan 1, 2026): generative AI developers
  must post a summary of their training data. xAI sued; a federal judge
  refused to block the law (March 2026); Ninth Circuit argument is set
  for Nov 18, 2026.
  Source: https://www.recordinglaw.com/us-laws/ai-laws/california-ai-laws/
- California SB 243 (in force Jan 1, 2026): companion chatbots must tell
  known minors they are talking to AI, remind them to take a break at
  least every 3 hours, and follow crisis protocols.
  Source: https://calawyers.org/privacy-law/regulatory-focus-on-ai-companion-character-chatbots/
- Texas HB 149 / TRAIGA (in force Jan 1, 2026): bans certain harmful AI
  uses and requires disclosures. The Texas attorney general has
  exclusive enforcement, must give a 60-day cure period, and there is
  no private right to sue. Penalties $10k-$200k per violation plus
  daily amounts for continuing violations. The AG had to launch a
  public online complaint portal by Sept 1, 2026.
  Source: https://casrai.org/guides/texas-traiga-explained
- Colorado: the original Colorado AI Act (SB 24-205) NEVER took effect.
  Timeline: delayed from Feb 1 to June 30, 2026 -> xAI sued (Apr 9,
  2026) -> federal magistrate stayed enforcement (Apr 27) -> repealed
  and replaced by SB 26-189, signed May 14, 2026. SB 26-189 is a
  narrower disclosure law for automated decisions in areas like jobs,
  loans, housing and insurance, effective Jan 1, 2027, enforced by the
  attorney general only, with a 60-day cure period. AG rulemaking
  hearing held Oct 26, 2026; final rules due by Jan 1, 2027.
  Sources: https://securityboulevard.com/2026/11/us-state-ai-laws-in-force-what-an-enterprise-deployer-actually-owes/
           https://stackcyber.com/posts/ai-colorado-laws
           https://intellisee.com/intelligence/colorado-ai-act-sb-24-205-repeal-2026-compliance-briefing/
- New York City Local Law 144 (enforced since July 2023): annual
  independent bias audit of AI hiring tools, public audit summary, and
  candidate notice; up to $1,500 per violation per day. The employer is
  responsible even if it bought the tool from a vendor. A December 2025
  New York State Comptroller audit found the city's complaint-driven
  enforcement was weak (only two AEDT complaints received in the audit
  period).
  Sources: https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/12/02/enforcement-local-law-144-automated-employment-decision-tools
           https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/critical-audit-of-nyc-ai-hiring-law-signals-increased-risk-for-employers
- New York RAISE Act (signed Dec 19, 2025, rewritten Mar 27, 2026,
  effective Jan 1, 2027): frontier developers only; safety incidents
  must be reported within 72 hours (California SB 53: 15 days).
  Developer registration begins Nov 2026.
  Sources: https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/new-york-finalizes-raise-act-for-8270433/
           https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/new-york-ai-law-2026-guide

  Sources: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/24/openai-agent-hacked-australian-government-website-.html

  Sources: https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/artificial-intelligence/openais-ai-agents-accidentally-uploaded-user-provided-images-to-third-party-sites

  Sept: California opens a formal investigation.
  Sept 30: California subpoena served (announced Oct 1). A subpoena

  Sources: https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/2026-10-01-california-ag-subpoena-openai-agents

4. PERSONAS
======================================================================
PERSONA 1 - SMALL COMPANY (DECIDED)
Who: startups and small firms using AI in hiring, lending, healthcare,
chatbots or content generation, selling across state lines, with no
law firm on retainer.
Need: "Which AI rules apply to me, in which states, and what must I do?"

PERSONA 2 - CITIZEN (DECIDED)
Who: an ordinary resident near existing or proposed data centers.
Need: "Is a data center near me raising my bills, and what can I do?"
Uses: the data center watchdog.

PERSONA 3 - STATE ATTORNEY GENERAL INVESTIGATOR (DECIDED)
Who: an investigator in a state attorney general's office.
Why this persona: most state AI laws are enforced by state attorneys
general, not police (e.g. Texas AG for TRAIGA, Colorado AG for SB
26-189; several AGs separately investigated OpenAI after the Hugging
Face incident).
Situation: a small team covering a whole state, relying on complaints
and tips because it can't watch every company.
Need: "Which companies may be breaking state AI or data laws, and what
evidence do we have?"
Uses: violation pattern detector (F19), evidence pack builder (F20),
cross-state comparison (F21), data flow traces (F9).

How the three personas connect:
- Citizen files a complaint (F17).
- Investigator sees patterns across complaints (F19) and builds a case
  (F20).
- Small company gets alerts (F5) and fixes problems before an
  investigator calls.
Analogy: drivers, a traffic GPS and traffic police, all reading the
same rulebook.

5. FEATURES
======================================================================
F1. LAW TRACKER (core)
Pulls new AI bills and signed laws from state legislature sites and
tracks status: introduced, signed, delayed, challenged in court,
repealed/replaced.

F2. OBLIGATION EXTRACTOR (core)
An LLM reads each law and turns it into structured rules: who it covers,
what triggers it, what you must do, by when, and the penalty. A
lawyer-reviewed sample checks extraction accuracy.

F3. PRODUCT PROFILER (core, persona 1)
Company describes its AI features in plain language or connects product
docs. Tool classifies each feature, e.g. "resume screening",
"chatbot for under-18s", "generates images".

F4. APPLICABILITY ENGINE (core, persona 1)
Matches each feature against each state where the company has users and
produces a per-state checklist.

F5. CHANGE ALERTS (core, persona 1)
When a law is signed, delayed, challenged or replaced, tells the
company exactly which features are affected.

F6. DOCUMENT DRAFTER (core, persona 1)
Drafts required notices, disclosures and impact assessment templates
per state.

F7. INCIDENT RESPONSE MODE (proposed)
When an AI system causes harm:
- for the company whose AI caused it: which state laws require
  reporting the incident and by when;
- for the victim: which state data breach rules apply, who must be
  notified, and a draft notice.

F8. DATA USAGE LAW COVERAGE (decided in scope)
Extend F1-F6 beyond AI laws to state privacy laws and state data breach
notification laws (every state has one, each with different rules).

F9. DATA FLOW TRACER (proposed, owner persona not decided)
Watches where an AI tool or agent actually sends data and compares it
against what the company's privacy policy promises.
Analogy: checking your receipt against what actually left your account.
Motivated by the Hugging Face incident and the 53 uploaded images.

F10. DATA CENTER WATCHDOG (decided, MINOR, persona 2)
Citizen view: map of existing and proposed data centers, how much of
a household bill comes from data center demand, alerts for local
hearings.
Evidence (PJM grid, 13 states plus DC):
- Data centers are over 90% of PJM's forecast new power demand (PJM
  long-term forecast: about 94%).
- Capacity auction price rose from $28.92 to $329.17/MW-day in two
  years (about +1,000%); the 2027/28 auction cleared at $333.44.
- The independent market monitor attributed 63% of the 2025/26 price
  jump to data centers.
- ICF projects home rates could rise 30-60% by 2030.
Sources: https://tr.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025:newsml_L1N3TP0R8:0-power-costs-soar-in-pjm-region-as-data-center-demand-spikes/
         https://ieefa.org/resources/projected-data-center-growth-spurs-pjm-capacity-prices-factor-10
         https://introl.com/ar/blog/pjm-rate-shock-100-billion-data-center-electricity-2026

5b. ADDITIONAL FEATURES
======================================================================
FOR THE SMALL COMPANY (persona 1 - PRIORITY)
F11. AI VENDOR CHECKER
Before buying an AI tool, the company enters the vendor's name and sees
which laws that tool could trigger in its states.
Analogy: checking a used car's history before buying it.

F12. PLAIN ENGLISH EXPLAINER
Any law explained in about five beginner-level sentences, with a link
to the original text.

F13. COMPLIANCE SCORE PER STATE
Traffic-light view: green (compliant), yellow (action needed soon),
red (non-compliant now).

F14. DEADLINE CALENDAR
All upcoming effective dates and filing deadlines in one calendar,
with reminders.

F15. "WHAT IF WE EXPAND?" SIMULATOR
"We're launching in Colorado next month. What changes?" Shows new
obligations before launch.

FOR THE CITIZEN (persona 2)
F16. "WAS AI USED ON ME?" CHECKER
Someone rejected for a job or loan sees which state laws gave them a
right to be told AI was used, plus a draft letter asking the company.

F17. COMPLAINT FILER
Turns a citizen's story into a properly formatted complaint to the
right state attorney general's office (e.g. the Texas AG's online
complaint portal required by TRAIGA).

F18. DATA CENTER HEARING ALERTS
Notification when a data center proposal nearby goes to a public
hearing, with a summary of what's being decided.

FOR THE STATE ATTORNEY GENERAL INVESTIGATOR (persona 3)
F19. VIOLATION PATTERN DETECTOR
Spots when many complaints point at the same company or AI tool.

F20. EVIDENCE PACK BUILDER
Bundles complaints, data flow traces (F9) and relevant law text into
one case file.

F21. CROSS-STATE COMPARISON
Shows how the same company is treated in different states.

TIED TO THE HUGGING FACE HOOK
F22. AI INCIDENT TIMELINE REPLAYER
Feed in an incident's events; shows hour by hour who had to report
what, to whom, and by when. Candidate for a live demo moment.

Note: demo scope still open. Judges usually reward a few features done
well over many half-built ones.

8. FIT AGAINST THE FIVE CRITERIA
======================================================================
1. Not an ordinary AI app: STRONG.
2. US-specific: VERY STRONG (exists because there is no federal law).
3. Audience hasn't considered it: MEDIUM (lawyers know; founders and
   public mostly don't).
4. Long-standing: WEAK (mainly 2024 onward, but growing fast).
   New evidence for the team to weigh: NYC Local Law 144 has been
   enforced since 2023, and a December 2025 State Comptroller audit
   found the city agency struggled to enforce it. Rating not changed.
5. Not already solved: MEDIUM (law firm trackers exist, mostly for big
   clients).

======================================================================
9. RISKS
======================================================================
- Laws change constantly (Colorado's AI law was replaced before it
  ever took effect); the tool must never claim to give legal advice.
- LLM extraction errors could say a company is compliant when it isn't;
  every output must link to the law's actual text.
- SB 53 and New York's RAISE Act only cover the largest model
  developers, so for most startups the incident angle applies to them
  as victims, not developers.
- Scope creep: F7-F10 could make the demo too broad for a hackathon.
